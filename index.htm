Make the pattern grow

A sequence is an ordered list of terms. n tells us the position; an tells us the value at that position. Here, positions begin at 1.

PREDICT → CHECK → EXPLAIN

Your pattern mission

  1. Compare positions 2, 3 and 4. What changes? What stays the same?
  2. Predict the value at position 8 before moving the slider there.
  3. Explain how you could find position 20 without drawing every tile.
Reveal the rule and a visual explanation

Two different kinds of rule

Term-to-term: how to move from one term to the next.

Position-to-term / 通項: how to calculate a term directly from its position.

A few terms do not determine a unique sequence. For 1, 2, 4, one possible next term is 8 if we double. Another is 7 if we add 1, then 2, then 3. Always explain your assumed rule.
SENIOR-SECONDARY BRIDGE / 延伸

Be a sequence designer

Keep the first term fixed. Compare adding the same number with multiplying by the same number.

Displayed experiment

Horizontal axis: position n. Each dot is a term. Connecting lines are visual guides, not extra terms. Vertical scales change between experiments.

Experiment cards

  1. Growing: Start at 2 and add 3. Explain why a₁₀ is not 2 + 10 × 3.
  2. Decreasing: Start at 10 and add −2. At which position is the term zero?
  3. Constant: Try d = 0, then r = 1. What stays unchanged?
  4. Alternating: Start at 2 and multiply by −2. Describe the signs.
  5. Shrinking: Start at 16 and multiply by 0.5. Does a displayed small value mean the term is exactly zero?
Senior bridge: general terms and sums

Arithmetic: aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

There are n − 1 jumps from the first term to the nth term.

Geometric: aₙ = a₁ × rⁿ⁻¹

There are n − 1 multiplications.

Arithmetic sum: Sₙ = n(a₁ + aₙ)/2.

Geometric sum: Sₙ = a₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r), when r ≠ 1. When r = 1, Sₙ = na₁.

Mini-investigation: With a₁ = 2, compare “add 3” and “multiply by 2”. Find the first position where the second sequence is larger. Explain using a table, not just the graph.
LOW-STAKES PRACTICE / 小試牛刀

Explain it to yourself

Success checklist

  • I can distinguish a position from a term value.
  • I can describe a term-to-term rule.
  • I can substitute n into a given formula.
  • I remember that n = 1 gives the first term.
  • I can explain an answer instead of only guessing.

Teacher guide / 教師使用建議

Suggested 40-minute lesson: 5 min vocabulary and noticing; 12 min visual missions; 10 min sequence lab; 8 min practice; 5 min exit discussion.

Enrichment: Allocate another 25–40 minutes or a short home project. Students download their reports and submit through your school's usual platform.

Support: Start with even numbers. Say “position” and “value” aloud. Use the first four pictures before showing notation.

Stretch: Explain the triangular-number formula by arranging two copies into a rectangle. Investigate geometric sums in the lab.

Exit question: “A student says the next term of 2, 4, 6 must be 8. What assumption have they made?”
